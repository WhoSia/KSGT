# R7h — Foregrounding is not maximal ease or maximal surprise
Miall & Kuiken (1994), Poetics 22:389–407, DOI 10.1016/0304-422X(94)00011-5. Author university PDF https://sites.ualberta.ca/~dmiall/LiteraryReading/Readings/Miall%20Kuiken%20Foregrounding.pdf . Examined abstract, rationale and studies/discussion text; PDF screenshot failed cache, not a verified local byte acquisition. Four studies across three literary stories, segmented reading/rating tasks; reported association with slower reading, strikingness and affect. This is evidence under elicited conditions, not a naturalistic gold or causal proof that deviations improve writing. Genre and story dependence; reading time is a coarse proxy.
Transfer: lexical surprise may support a delayed relation that becomes interpretable later, but unrelated novelty is incoherence; easy prediction can be appropriate in notices. Avoid automatic smoothness, surprise, syntactic length or affect as scalar quality. Candidate generation should retain a distinctive detail and let its interpretation emerge where licensed, not add obligatory metaphor, needless oddity, plot twist, or explanatory coda. Research question: can distinguish required resolution from deliberately open interpretation and preserve both at end of actual text?
