Study the opening and the transition into the travel companion's account, not the entire work or an authoritative edition. The opening narrator treats unfamiliar clothing and talk as signs of an irritating stranger; the interlocutor's later questions about work and the account of dispossession alter what the earlier detail signifies. The later context retrospectively changes an interpretation without changing the previously described physical facts. The text also explicitly narrates a change of attitude in places, so it is not evidence that literary prose must avoid explicit interpretation. This is close reading of one natural authored work, not a reader experiment, not corpus statistics, not author-intent gold, and no copied text transmitted to the provider. 
Transfer hypothesis: a continuation can reveal context, preserving concrete details while changing their significance; open and resolved interpretations are legitimate alternatives conditioned on the intended work, not a universal minimal-explanation rule.
