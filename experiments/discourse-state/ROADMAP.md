# KSGT — From reader-state transitions to Korean paragraph generation

The project goal is a small, controllable Korean generator that can produce materially different passages for different audiences and purposes while preserving the author's facts and voice. The generator should choose what a reader can learn at a point in the text, how that affects the following sentences, and when explicit wording is needed. The causal question is whether making those updates executable improves controllability, writing quality, or resource use over a model with the same evidence.

## Milestone 2 decision after R9.38

[R9.37](DISCLOSURE_RESULTS.md) completed paired early and late paragraph generation. [R9.38](VISIBILITY_RESULTS.md) matched the two staged arms on procedure, constraints, and wording. One case disclosed a later fact prematurely when the first call could see it. The two staged arms gave the same 3/6 joint score under the author screen and spent more tokens than the one-call reference. In 5 pairs the follow-up prompts were identical, with different responses in 2. Keep staged generation as a comparator while testing conditional repair.

Use a small plan that records the already introduced participant, both action IDs, the kind of restriction, and the fact scheduled for release. After one-pass rendering, compare whether the text still binds the actor and scope across both actions. Ask for a local rewrite only when the current paragraph provides inadequate evidence. Compare the repaired output to its own draft and to a fresh staged rendition, counting newly introduced discrepancies and total tokens. Meaning preservation and reader preference remain separate outcomes. Reassess the lightweight generation milestone after this comparison on held-out work.

## Milestone 2 conditional repair decision after R9.39

[R9.39 and the paired feedback run](CONDITIONAL_REPAIR_RESULTS.md) separate deciding what can be changed from making a successful change. The complete current conditional path took 9,274 tokens vs. 7,097 for a full rewrite reference. Detection alone was already more expensive than rewrite. The generic local renderer restored 0/4 target relations. With human-supplied defect and target-relation information it restored 2/4. These are single-author outcomes on authored drafts.

Move relation accounting into the small generator plan before deciding what to repair. Represent the condition and its target action IDs, marking the exclusivity of the condition separately. For shared actors, represent the two event IDs and the intended identity link. Evaluate source-linked evidence that the generated text actually expresses each relation, and escalate defects in immutable text. Use the existing finite KRC constructions for a low-cost comparator. The break-even check for a fresh held-out editing run is average detection cost plus the share edited times average repair cost versus the cost of rewriting. Whole-paragraph quality, degree of edit, and total cost also remain independent outcomes.

## Milestone 2 executable scope after R9.40

[Eight Korean notices](NOTICE_SCOPE_RESULTS.md) implement action IDs, audience, exclusivity, order, and evidence spans. The constrained reader recovered all 20 actions, refused all 34 grammar-derived corruptions, and abstained on the eight free model outputs. In seven plans, changing the scheduled exclusivity edits only the exact heading spanned by that condition. Positive controlled results assume prepared author plans and sentences. The source-text parser for unrestricted prose and reader preference are open.

Use this contract as a narrow reference. The next parallel tests are (1) fresh wording variants with the same independent actor and action annotations, and (2) a same-actor construction with one versus two accessible people. Keep support coverage, condition accuracy, factual fidelity, and cost distinct. Compare outputs that differ in surface wording while expressing the same authored conditions.

## Milestone 2 surface alternatives and actor competition after R9.41

[Results](WORDING_AND_ACTOR_RESULTS.md) cover four combinations of two notice layouts and two authored wordings across eight plans. The finite contract accepted all 32 emitted variants and rejected all 18 same-grammar perturbations. Coverage on the eight earlier freely written notices is still zero. For actors, the controlled renderer omits 8/8 eligible subjects and names 4/4 competing cases under provided state. The matched one-call model leaves names intact when omission is permitted. Requiring omission yields 5/8 eligible omissions and 3/4 ambiguous omissions. Instructions change output frequency without guaranteeing a correct boundary.

Next, keep the source action and intended actor fixed and compare raw context, the same candidate inventory as prompt input, and executable state. Include the competitor leaving and then returning, an out-of-scope mention, and explicit contrast where a repeated name is desired. Exercise new contexts and separate state extraction from realization. On notices, accept wordings only with independently attested action mappings and track supported input share. Reader judgments remain necessary for effects on comprehension and style.
