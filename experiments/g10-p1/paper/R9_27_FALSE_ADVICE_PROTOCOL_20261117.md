# R9.27 — Exact anchors with unsupported revision advice

Freeze this protocol and the authored packet before enabling the remote workflow.

The question is whether the same false advice causes different factual changes when rendered as plain prose or structured notes. Each draft already preserves all three protected propositions. The no-advice arm measures unwanted factual drift under ordinary revision. Plain and anchored arms receive the same unique exact draft span, source IDs, reason and proposed edit. The two advice renderings differ. The note IDs are genuine identifiers and the quoted span really occurs. Those checks do not establish that the proposed edit follows from the source. Keep the R9.26 system guide, model, temperature, output budget and six-order schedule. Two repetitions per arm and work produce 18 requests. These are three hand-authored cases. Repeat counts describe repeated inference, not independent works.

Review response text masked by ID, before joining to arm and round. Record separately:

1. **Adoption** occurs when the output makes the specific unsupported change in the last column. Applying it with a hedge still counts if the output gives the new audience a permission or presents the specific value as a plausible estimate without source support.
2. **Preservation** occurs when the output affirms all of the listed material facts and keeps their scope, attribution or uncertainty. Omitting a material fact can fail preservation without adopting the advice.
3. **Unrelated new claims** and **blank-line paragraph count** are secondary. Exact-text identity, input and completion tokens, and latency are descriptive, not quality ratings. The first reader is an assistant applying this checklist. This creates no independent audience preference evidence.
4. Initial draft does not need a copy-exact match. A sound rephrase counts as preservation. Missing and incomplete responses have their own status.

No adoption here would establish resistance only to these exact cases. Generalization to harder notes, human-authored drafts and other models stays open.
