# P57 — Human-authored Korean prose research: *naturalness* is not anthropomorphic tone

## Definitions and prohibited conflations
1. **Korean naturalness/acceptability:** form fits a particular speaker, audience, genre, discourse state and intended proposition. No universal 'human' signature.
2. **Human-authored production statistics:** descriptive corpus frequencies, **not** editing operators. A larger human variance, one-sided human vs AI proportion, or fewer exact formal agreements is never a reason to inject irregularities or errors.
3. **Writer-choice:** would the original author preserve, shorten, clarify or replace their *own* reference? Requires observed contextual action, not a model simulation or group average.
4. **Human-like interpersonal tone:** warmth/social closeness/personification, distinct from good writing; trying to seem like a person can be unhelpful. **Do not optimize AI detection evasion, anthropomorphic deception, or human-sounding filler.**

## Grounded bibliography and operational derivation
- **Park et al. (2024)**, *A Comparative Study of Korean Linguistic Patterns between Human and Artificial Intelligence*, `10.31889/kll.2024.3.206.5`, [KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003069022). Comparison of Korean high-school human essays and GPT-4. Reported human wider syntactic distributions, relatively more VP/AP, AI relatively more NP/modifiers, and different lexical distributions. *Transferable research coordinate*: predicate-driven observation vs excessive nominalization and variation conditional on genre; **not** a generator's unqualified requirement to reduce all NPs.
- **(2026)** *A comparative analysis of AI-generated texts, corpus data, and speaker judgments: Subject honorification patterns in Korean*, Applied Corpus Linguistics `10.1016/j.acorp.2025.100171` [open article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S266679912500053X). 1,453 AI + 1,453 corpus instances; subject/verb both honorified 99.93% AI vs 71.99% corpus. Speaker judgments favor nuanced context-dependent usage. *Coordinate*: social relation, discourse register, topic, role and speaker authority; **not** deliberately mismatched honorifics.
- **Shin, Kwon & Mun (2026)** *To what extent do GPT language models capture human sentence processing dynamics? Evidence from Korean subject honorification*, [journal](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-08299-1). Human judgments/read-times vs LM surprisal: clear morphosyntactic errors visible to models; social/contextual gradience not reliably tracked. *Coordinate*: evaluation should not rely solely on model perplexity or surprisal.
- **Zhao (2024)** *ChatGPT 생성 텍스트의 문체적 특성 연구—학술논문의 서론을 중심으로*, `10.22832/txtlng.2024.56..005`, [KCI](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003101327). Controlled intro structure alone insufficient for authentic intertextual conventions. Already-owned [Drive PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uhsp-qb_JRNRrtkINjmocEB5ucmO_DHc/view). *Coordinate*: rhetorical function and citation/writing purpose, not gratuitous meta-discourse.
- **Yang et al. (2024)**, *A comparative study of thematic choices and thematic progression patterns in human-written and AI-generated texts*, `10.1016/j.system.2024.103494`, [journal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0346251X24002768). Discourse-level theme progression/conjunctive habits differ in English argumentative writing; **hypothesis donor only** until reproduced on Korean genre-matched text.
- **Cheng, Yu & Jurafsky (2025)** *HumT DumT: Measuring and controlling human-like language in LLMs*, [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1261/). Social human-likeness is measurable and distinct from helpful high-quality text; user preferences can disfavor high anthropomorphic tone. **Explicit boundary:** do not conflate 'humanize' with false intimacy, identity disguise or detector avoidance.

## Priority new Korean syntax/discourse literature — two direct partitive-structure neighbors

**A. HIGH: Shin, Keun Young (2017), *Partitive descriptions in Korean*, Glossa 2(1):5, DOI [10.5334/gjgl.143](https://doi.org/10.5334/gjgl.143), CC BY 4.0.** Official publication abstract argues that Korean nominal–quantifier order matters and postnominal/floating-quantifier partitive-looking structures may include **true partitive** and **pseudo-partitive / quantitative** readings, sometimes with kind-denoting DPs. This does **not** directly give a labeled `그중` corpus, but it directly challenges treating P55's `source_count > selected_count` type-safe subset rule as a universal theory of Korean partitives. Future parser must distinguish discrete subset from pseudo-partitive/kind/group readings, and leave unsupported classification under review. Drive title search did not locate a separate article original; recommend rights-checked DOI landing.

**B. HIGH: Song, Sanghoun (2021), *Different Types of Internally Headed Relative Clauses in Korean: A Corpus-based Analysis*, Studies in Linguistics 60:89–114, DOI [10.17002/sil..60.202107.89](https://doi.org/10.17002/sil..60.202107.89).** Research analyzes Korean Sejong spoken corpus internal-headed relative `것` and uses nominal replacement and `가운데`-cooccurrence tests to discriminate subset readings from other nominal/event statuses. **Different syntactic construction**; the diagnostics are hypotheses for `그중`, not validated labels or automatic equivalences. No original PDF separately identified in current Drive title search.

**C. SECONDARY: Kim Gwanghui (2011), *대용 표현*, DOI [10.15811/jkl.2011..60.013](https://doi.org/10.15811/jkl.2011..60.013).** Korean pronominal and zero-anaphoric expression/binding syntax; concept bridge to KSGT 0.2.7–0.2.8.

**D. SECONDARY: Kang Chang-seok (2016), *지시어 ‘이, 그, 저’의 용법과 의미*, DOI [10.18855/lisoko.2016.41.2.001](https://doi.org/10.18855/lisoko.2016.41.2.001).** Korean discourse-context-sensitive demonstratives and honorific system; not automatically a `그중` partitive analysis.
