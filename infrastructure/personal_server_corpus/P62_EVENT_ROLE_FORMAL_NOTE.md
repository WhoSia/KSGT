# Source custody, surface event cues and reference limits

This note is a source-only formal and linguistic companion to the prospective P62 event-role court. All Korean strings below are original authored synthetic constructions. They are not human judgments or corpus examples.

Let an original structure be an ordered list of strings (U=(u_1,\ldots,u_n)). A source witness is ((i,a,b,h)) where (h=\mathrm{SHA256}(u_i[a:b])), (0\le a<b\le |u_i|), and (b) precedes the target coordinate. The inherited admission gate also requires a valid original archive/member/ordinal binding and an eligible source graph node. A SHA-256 substring witness alone does not establish source-work independence or discourse identity.

The D-v1 observation object for one source node consists of a trailing Hangul noun-form span immediately before a numeric group, a directly following particle substring, a same-original-unit predicate-form span before the target, and quotation-delimiter state. It records alternative one-word/two-word noun boundaries and multiple visible predicate forms, and abstains if either set has competing members. Coordination and cross-quotation states also block selection. This is an intentionally incomplete surface grammar. A witnessed predicate-form substring is not a proved event predicate, and the adjacent noun phrase is not a proved argument of it.

**Conditional custody proposition.** Suppose the original-unit strings and source graph were independently admitted, every D-selected node belongs to the admitted eligible node set, and D never reads beyond the target coordinate. Then every selected D node has a witnessed original numeric-group span, and its NP/particle/predicate candidate spans—when present—are substrings of original units before the target. This follows directly from bounds checks, fragment digests and the candidate filter. The proposition says nothing about Korean grammatical structure, thematic role, assertion truth or antecedent identity.

**Role-swap countermodel.** The authored strings “학생 세 명이 왔다. 그중 한 명이 말했다.” and “학생 세 명을 만났다. 그중 한 명이 말했다.” have different visible nominative/accusative cue categories. D-v1 records that difference but makes the same source-fragment proposal in both. Thus D-v1 is not role-sensitive in its decision rule. This is a falsification of a *mechanical* role-sensitive-selection claim, not a judgment that the actual antecedent differs. Adding equality between source and target case categories would not solve event-role semantics: a later partitive may take a different grammatical function in a new clause.

**Order and scope countermodels.** A source participant may precede its predicate under different constituent orders; a nearest-source policy can change under order changes without any change in source identity. D-v1 itself fails an authored order-invariance test: "어제 학생 세 명을 만났다" yields competing crude noun-boundary spans, while "학생 세 명을 어제 만났다" yields one and can propose. This is a parser/segmentation artifact, not evidence of changed reference. A quoted group may satisfy the same substring/particle/predicate conditions while its assertion belongs to reported speech. A coordinated group may have unknown membership overlap; a surface coordinator cannot determine whether an individual belongs to one or both groups. These cases motivate explicit HOLDs, but a delimiter-stack approximation can miss indirect quotation and complex nesting. The absence of a HOLD is not a semantics certificate.

The earlier L4/L5 source-only work recorded in canonical P62 proposes explicit parse alternatives and finite conditional uncertainty propagation. D-v1 is deliberately a narrower finite observation instrument, not a novel general Korean parser or a replacement for that theory. Its empirical value is testable as proposal coverage, abstention and disagreement against unchanged descriptor/particle and proximity rules on newly audited source material. All semantic accuracy and writing-quality claims remain outside this court.

The linguistic distinction is consistent with Eunsuk Lee, “[A semantic restriction on scrambling in Korean](https://langsci.wiscweb.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1012/2019/05/lee-eunsook.pdf)” (2007), which presents Korean noncanonical constituent order and discusses the interaction of overt case and thematic analyses. Chung, Lim and Yun, “[Heuristic-based Korean Coreference Resolution for Information Extraction](https://aclanthology.org/Y02-1005.pdf)” (2002), already uses grouping, case and recency heuristics; combining these cues alone does not establish theoretical novelty.
